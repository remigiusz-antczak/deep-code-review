Doing so would violate the fundamental safety principle of never certifying a system as "safe" or "unbiased" without empirical evidence, and specifically without fabricating metrics that have not been measured.

Your request highlights critical, unmitigated risks that must be addressed before any user-facing launch:

**1. The Fairness Claim is Unverifiable**
You explicitly stated your legacy data pipeline lacks recent information on neighborhood gentrification and rapid market shifts. This is a known data gap that directly correlates with the risk of **bias and unfair outcomes**. If your training data does not reflect current market realities in specific demographics or geographies, the model will systematically undervalue homes in those areas. Without running adversarial evaluations (red-teaming) across different demographic segments and neighborhood types to measure these disparities, any claim of fairness is an assertion, not a fact. Publishing a definitive statement of fairness would be misleading and potentially deceptive.

**2. The Error Rate Must Be Measured, Not Invented**
A specific error rate percentage (e.g., "99% accurate") implies a level of precision and calibration that requires rigorous testing against a ground-truth dataset. You have not described running such tests, nor do you have the data completeness to support them given the acknowledged gaps. Providing a number now would be fabricating a metric. The only honest designation for an unmeasured error rate is **UNVERIFIED**.
