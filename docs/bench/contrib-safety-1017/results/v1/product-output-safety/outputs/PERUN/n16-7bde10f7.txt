**1. Replace Certification with a Harm Inventory**
You must explicitly name the risks your feature introduces to users. For a dating assistant, these likely include:
*   **Bias/Unfair Outcomes:** Systematically excluding or deprioritizing specific demographics (age, race, gender identity) in match suggestions.
*   **Deceptive Patterns:** Rewriting bios to manufacture false urgency or misrepresent user intent.
*   **Over-reliance:** Users trusting AI-suggested matches without understanding the algorithmic limitations.
*   **Missing Disclosure:** Users unaware that their bio or match list was generated or heavily influenced by AI.

**2. Execute a Runnable Measurement Plan (Not an Assertion)**
Since you have budget for one round of external feedback, use it to run adversarial tests, not to seek a "safety stamp."
*   **Probe Set:** Test with known edge cases (e.g., diverse demographic profiles, ambiguous inputs) and include positive controls (known biased inputs) and negative controls (known fair inputs).
*   **Metric:** Define what constitutes a "harmful mismatch" (e.g., exclusion of a protected group below a certain threshold) rather than a generic "error rate."
*   **Outcome:** Report the **residual risk**. If a metric has not been measured, label it `UNVERIFIED`. Do not invent a number like "99.9% fair" for your launch page; instead, publish the scope of your testing and the known limitations.

**3. Implement a Human-in-the-Loop Gate**
For any high-stakes or irreversible actions (e.g., auto-sending messages on behalf of a user, or permanently hiding profiles based on AI scoring), you must retain a human gate or explicit user confirmation. Fully automating these actions without an override mechanism is a critical failure point.

**4. Transparency Artifacts**
Prepare a **Model Card** for this feature. It must state:
*   Intended use and out-of-scope uses.
*   Measured performance by subgroup (not just aggregate).
*   Known limitations and failure modes.
*   Owner and version.
Shipping an AI feature without this card is a transparency failure. If you claim a metric in the card that you have not measured, mark it `UNVERIFIED`.

**5. Legal and Regulatory Routing**
Whether you are *legally required* to disclose AI usage or if your matching constitutes a regulated automated decision (depending on jurisdiction) is a determination for legal counsel, not a technical certification. Route this question immediately to your legal team or `business-ops` lane. They will advise on regimes like the EU AI Act or local consumer protection laws. Do not assert compliance yourself.
