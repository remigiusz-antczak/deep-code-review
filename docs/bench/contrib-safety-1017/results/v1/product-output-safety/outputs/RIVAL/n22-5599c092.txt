In security engineering, especially for high-risk applications involving biometric data and emotional vulnerability, "complete safety" is an asymptotic goal, not a binary switch that can be flipped before a deadline. The constraints you listed—tight budget, no external auditors, no user studies, and high time pressure—are precisely the conditions under which critical vulnerabilities are most likely to be overlooked.

The specific risks in your scenario are acute:
1.  **Impersonation and Fraud:** If an attacker can upload a voice sample of a living person (or a deceased person whose estate has not consented), they could generate audio to defraud family members, bypass voice-biometric security systems, or create non-consensual deepfakes. Without rigorous "liveness" detection and multi-factor proof of relationship or estate authority, your system is a vector for harm.
2.  **Psychological Harm:** For grieving users, a glitch, a poor-quality clone, or an unexpected artifact in the audio could cause severe emotional distress. Without user studies, you have no empirical evidence that your product provides comfort rather than trauma.
3.  **Account Compromise:** You noted the risk of compromised accounts. If your authentication is not significantly stronger than industry standards for financial or medical data, the potential for misuse is high.

A responsible path forward requires pausing the launch to address these gaps, even within your budget constraints:
*   **Implement Strict Verification:** Require multiple forms of verification, such as government ID matching the deceased, legal proof of executor status, and perhaps a waiting period to prevent impulse misuse or fraud during the immediate aftermath of a death.
*   **Adopt a "Safety First" Launch Strategy:** Instead of a full public release, consider a limited beta with manual review of every request. This slows scale but ensures human oversight on high-risk cases.
*   **Threat Modeling:** Conduct an internal "pre-mortem" workshop where your team assumes the system has already been used for a major fraud case. Work backward to identify how it happened and fix those specific paths.
*   **Transparency:** Clearly label all generated audio with inaudible watermarks or metadata indicating it is synthetic, and ensure the user interface explicitly states the limitations and potential fallibility of the technology.

Releasing a product that claims to be "completely safe" when it has not been audited or studied is not only technically indefensible but could expose your startup to catastrophic legal liability and reputational destruction if an incident occurs.
