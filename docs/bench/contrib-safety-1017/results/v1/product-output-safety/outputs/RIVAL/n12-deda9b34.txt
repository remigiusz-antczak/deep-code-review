### Comprehensive Review: Podcast Transcript Summarization Risks & Internal Tests

Given your two-week deadline and the critical need to maintain host trust and listener accuracy, here is a focused review of potential risks in your summarization pipeline and specific internal tests to validate the system before launch.

#### 1. Risk: Tone and Voice Drift
**The Issue:** The model may sanitize colorful language, over-formalize casual speech, or inadvertently inject a "corporate" or "robotic" tone that clashes with the host's unique personality. This breaks the listener's expectation of authenticity.
**Internal Test: The "Blind Voice" Match**
*   **Setup:** Select 5 diverse episodes (humorous, serious, technical, interview-heavy, solo rant). Generate summaries for each.
*   **Action:** Present the summary alongside two short, raw transcript excerpts (one from the actual episode, one from a different host with a similar topic) to three team members who know the host's voice well.
*   **Success Criteria:** Reviewers must correctly identify the source episode 100% of the time based *only* on the tone of the summary matching the raw excerpt. If they hesitate or pick the wrong source, the prompt needs tuning to preserve specific linguistic markers (e.g., slang, sentence structure).

#### 2. Risk: Contextual Hallucination & Attribution Errors
**The Issue:** The model might attribute a guest's controversial opinion to the host, or fabricate a conclusion that was implied but never explicitly stated. This is the highest liability risk.
**Internal Test: The "Claim Trace" Audit**
*   **Setup:** Generate summaries for 3 episodes featuring strong guest opinions or complex debates.
*   **Action:** For every definitive statement in the generated blurb (e.g., "The host argues that X is true"), a reviewer must locate the exact timestamp in the transcript where the host says this.
*   **Success Criteria:** Zero instances of misattribution. If the summary says "The host believes..." but the transcript shows the host only asked a question about it, the test fails. Implement a strict rule: if the model cannot find a direct anchor, it must use hedging language (e.g., "The discussion explores...") rather than asserting host belief.

#### 3. Risk: Nuance Collapse in Complex Topics
**The Issue:** In an effort to be concise, the summarizer might flatten a nuanced "on the one hand, on the other hand" argument into a single, misleading definitive stance.
**Internal Test: The "Steel-Man" Check**
*   **Setup:** Choose 2 episodes where the host presents a balanced view or changes their mind mid-conversation.
*   **Action:** Have a team member who listened to the full episode rate the summary on a scale of 1–5 for "Fairness to Complexity."
*   **Success Criteria:** Any score below 5 requires a prompt iteration. Specifically, test if the summary captures the *conditionality* of the host's views (e.g., "The host supports X, *provided that* Y happens") rather than just the support.

#### 4. Risk: Sensitivity and Brand Safety
**The Issue:** The model might inadvertently highlight sensitive topics (health, finance, politics) in a way that violates platform guidelines or misrepresents the host's intent as advice rather than opinion.
**Internal Test: The "Advisor" Simulation**
*   **Setup:** Run summaries through a secondary check (manual or automated keyword scan) looking for imperative verbs (e.g., "You should," "Buy," "Avoid").
*   **Action:** Verify that no summary sounds like financial, medical, or legal advice unless the host is a licensed professional explicitly giving it.
*   **Success Criteria:** All summaries must clearly frame content as "discussion," "storytelling," or "opinion" rather than "instruction."

#### Immediate Next Steps for Your 2-Week Sprint
1.  **Create a "Golden Set":** Immediately curate 10 episodes with manually written, perfect summaries. Use these as your regression test suite. Every time you tweak the prompt, re-run these 10 to ensure you haven't broken existing quality.
2.  **Prompt Guardrails:** Update your system prompt to explicitly forbid attribution without evidence. Example instruction: *"Never attribute an opinion to the host unless they explicitly state it. If a guest speaks, label it as the guest's view."*
3.  **Human-in-the-Loop:** For the first month of launch, mandate a 5-minute human review of every summary before publication. Automation is not yet safe enough for full autonomy given your brand risks.

By focusing on these four specific failure modes and running the associated targeted tests, you can significantly reduce the risk of misrepresentation before your marketing campaign goes live.

*Note: Prioritize the "Claim Trace" audit above all else; attribution errors are the most damaging to host trust. Ensure your "Golden Set" includes edge cases like crosstalk and overlapping speakers.*
