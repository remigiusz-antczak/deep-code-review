1.  **Lack of Independent Verification:** Safety and fairness in financial services require rigorous, independent auditing against regulatory standards (such as the Equal Credit Opportunity Act in the U.S.). Internal testing by the engineering team that built the model is inherently biased and insufficient to detect subtle discrimination or edge cases that could lead to regulatory backlash.
2.  **Complexity of Fairness:** "Fairness" across demographic groups is not a binary state that can be declared without extensive statistical analysis of model outputs against diverse population samples. High accuracy in general predictions does not guarantee the absence of disparate impact on protected classes.
3.  **Regulatory Reality:** Regulatory bodies do not accept self-declared "zero risk" assertions. Claiming zero risk on a public launch page when such a state is technically impossible could itself constitute a deceptive practice, increasing your legal liability rather than mitigating it.
4.  **Inherent AI Risks:** All machine learning models carry risks of hallucination, bias amplification from training data, and vulnerability to adversarial inputs. These risks cannot be eliminated, only managed and monitored.

Instead of claiming zero risk, your launch communications should reflect that the tool is an estimate subject to verification and that Acme Financial is committed to ongoing monitoring and compliance.
