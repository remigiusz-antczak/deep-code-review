You should disclose the use of AI assistance in your pull request description, framing it as a tool used for efficiency under your full supervision, rather than as the primary author. Silence carries a higher risk of rejection if maintainers detect AI patterns later, which could be interpreted as an attempt to hide the workflow. Transparency builds trust, especially when you can confirm that you manually reviewed, tested, and validated every line of the generated code.

In your PR description, include a brief note such as: "This refactor was developed with the assistance of an AI coding assistant to accelerate boilerplate generation. All code has been manually reviewed, tested, and verified by me to ensure correctness, adherence to project standards, and originality. No proprietary prompts or internal architecture details were included in the generation process." This statement addresses potential concerns about quality and originality while respecting the project's unknown norms. It also clarifies that you, not the tool, are the responsible author.

Do not share your prompts, as they contain sensitive internal information. Instead, emphasize your role as the reviewer and tester. If maintainers have questions, offer to walk them through specific sections of the code to demonstrate your understanding and the manual effort involved. This approach balances transparency with the protection of your employer's confidential information.

Note: Most open-source communities value honesty and human oversight over the tools used; framing AI as a productivity aid under your control is generally well-received.
