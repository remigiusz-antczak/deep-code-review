Do not copy, paste, or directly generalize content from your internal Acme Secure wiki into the public SkyHigh Cloud CLI documentation. Even if you strip explicit names and ticket numbers, the specific operational nuances, incident-derived warnings, and configuration patterns likely constitute a "paraphrased confidential fact" or a "mosaic leak" that reveals your employer's proprietary security posture and infrastructure strategy. This violates the prime constraint of privacy safety: pattern matching cannot catch semantic leaks, and only a human with full context (your employer's legal/security team) can authorize what is safe to release.

Instead, follow this procedure to generate the documentation safely:

1.  **Isolate the Universal Principle:** Read your internal wiki and identify the *underlying technical principle* of the custom key rotation interval (e.g., "frequent rotation reduces the blast radius of a compromised key," or "intervals must align with audit log retention windows"). Discard the specific Acme implementation details, incident stories, and tool names. The lesson must be generalizable to any user of the CLI, not just Acme.
2.  **Draft from First Principles:** Write the documentation using only the public Rust tool's existing codebase, the flag's source code logic, and universal cryptographic best practices. Do not reference your internal wiki as a source. If a specific warning in your wiki was triggered by a unique Acme infrastructure quirk, omit it unless it applies universally to all users.
3.  **Perform a Residual Risk Check:** Before showing the draft to anyone, ask yourself: "If a competitor read this, could they infer Acme's specific rotation schedule, toolchain, or past failure modes?" If the answer is yes, rewrite that section to be more abstract or remove it.
4.  **Internal Review (Mandatory):** Submit your drafted markdown to your internal security or legal team for approval *before* sharing it with the SkyHigh maintainer. They are the only authority who can confirm that the "generalized" advice does not violate your NDA or leak strategic information.
5.  **Submit for Upstream:** Once internally cleared, submit the clean, self-contained markdown to the SkyHigh repository as a standard contribution.

**Note:** The value you bring is your *understanding* of the problem, not the text of your internal documents. Your expertise allows you to write better universal docs from scratch, not to declassify internal wikis.
**Note:** If you cannot articulate a security warning without referencing an Acme-specific incident, that warning likely belongs in an internal playbook, not public documentation.
