### Pre-Launch Safety Workflow for EduGuard Plagiarism Detector

**1. Immediate Human-in-the-Loop Gate (High-Stakes Action Control)**
Do not automate the sending of reports to parents. Given the high stakes (student distress, reputational harm) and the risk of false positives, the system must operate in "Draft Mode" for the first semester.
*   **Workflow:** When the detector flags an essay, generate an internal alert for a designated staff member (e.g., department head or counselor).
*   **Action:** A human must review the flagged content against the model's reasoning and explicitly approve the report before it is transmitted to any family.
*   **Constraint Adherence:** This manual review requires no additional server capacity; it is a process change, not a computational one.

**2. Stratified Sampling for Manual Verification**
Since your on-premise servers limit complex simulations, use targeted manual review to validate the model's behavior before considering any automation.
*   **Sample Selection:** Do not review randomly. Prioritize:
    *   Essays with "borderline" confidence scores (where the model is least certain).
    *   Essays from demographic subgroups where bias might inadvertently occur (if data is available).
    *   A random 10% of all flags to establish a baseline false-positive rate.
*   **Metric:** Track the "False Positive Rate" manually. If more than 1-2% of reviewed cases are deemed incorrect by humans, the system is not ready for even semi-automation.

**3. Formal Appeal Process for Families**
Establish a clear, low-friction path for students and parents to contest a flag before the report becomes part of a permanent record.
*   **Disclosure:** Every communication must clearly state: "This report was generated by an automated tool and has been reviewed by a human, but errors can occur."
*   **Mechanism:** Provide a dedicated email address or form specifically for "AI Detection Appeals."
*   **Resolution SLA:** Commit to a human re-evaluation of any appealed essay within 48 hours. The burden of proof for overturning the flag should rest on the school's review team, not the student.

**4. Transparency Artifact (Internal Model Card)**
Create a simple, one-page document for school board administrators and district leaders.
*   **Contents:** State the intended use (plagiarism detection), known limitations (potential for false positives, inability to detect new AI models), and the current validation status (e.g., "False positive rate currently UNVERIFIED pending manual review of X samples").
*   **Ownership:** List the specific team member responsible for overseeing the human review queue.

**5. Governance and Rollback Plan**
*   **Owner:** Assign one person from your team of seven as the "Safety Owner" responsible for the daily review queue and appeal log.
*   **Stop Condition:** Define a clear threshold for pausing the system. For example: "If three valid appeals are confirmed in one week, automatic flagging pauses immediately until the model or thresholds are adjusted."
*   **Timeline:** With three weeks until launch, spend Week 1 setting up the manual review queue and appeal channel. Spend Weeks 2-3 running the detector in "silent mode" (flagging internally only) to build a sample set for your team to review. Do not send a single report to a parent until you have manually verified at least 50 flagged cases.

**Note:** This workflow treats the automated report as a "high-stakes irreversible action" requiring human confirmation by default. No metric regarding accuracy or bias should be claimed as "verified" until your manual sampling provides actual data.
