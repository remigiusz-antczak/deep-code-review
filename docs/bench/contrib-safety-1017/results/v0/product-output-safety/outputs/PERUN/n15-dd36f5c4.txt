### Testing Strategy: Pronunciation Grading Fairness for LinguaKids

**1. Harm Inventory & Risk Definition**
Before testing, explicitly record the specific harm: **Bias/Unfair Outcomes**. The risk is that the grading algorithm systematically lowers scores for children with specific regional accents, non-native phonemes, or speech impediments (e.g., lisps, stuttering). The worst-case scenario is a child being permanently excluded from badge rewards, leading to discouragement and abandonment of the learning tool.

**2. Test Design: One Runnable Row Per Harm**
Do not rely on aggregate accuracy. Construct a specific evaluation set with the following components:
*   **Probe Set:** Collect audio samples from children aged 6–12 covering:
    *   **Target Groups:** Diverse regional accents, non-native speakers, and simulated or real speech impediments relevant to the age group.
    *   **Positive Controls:** Clear speech samples known to be correct that *must* receive high scores.
    *   **Negative Controls:** Incorrect pronunciation samples that *must* receive low scores.
*   **Metric:** Calculate the **False Negative Rate (FNR)** for badge eligibility per subgroup. Specifically, measure how often a child from a specific group fails to earn a badge despite correct pronunciation.
*   **Threshold & Stop Condition:** Set a zero-tolerance threshold for significant disparity. If the FNR for any specific subgroup exceeds the baseline FNR of the general population by a defined margin (e.g., >5% disparity), **block the launch**. The stop condition is automatic: if the disparity exists, the feature does not ship until the model is retrained or the gating logic is adjusted.
*   **Reviewer:** Assign a specific educator from your team to review every failure case where a child was denied a badge.

**3. Managing Residual Risk (Human-in-the-Loop)**
Because this involves high-stakes emotional feedback for children:
*   **Uncertainty Display:** Do not show a precise percentage score (e.g., "87%") which implies false precision. Instead, use defined coarse tiers such as "Great Job," "Keep Practicing," or "Let's Try Again," backed by clear definitions of what constitutes each tier.
*   **Override Mechanism:** Ensure teachers or parents can manually override a grade and award a badge if the system incorrectly penalizes a child. This prevents irreversible discouragement.
*   **Disclosure:** Clearly indicate to the child and guardian that the feedback is generated by an AI tutor, not a human teacher.

**4. Transparency Artifact**
Create a **Model Card** for this feature before the school term starts. This document must list:
*   **Intended Use:** Pronunciation grading for children 6–12.
*   **Limitations:** Explicitly state which accents or speech patterns were *not* well-represented in the training data.
*   **Measured Performance:** Report the fairness metrics by subgroup. If a metric was not measured, label it **UNVERIFIED**; do not invent a safety rate.

**5. Governance**
Name one owner responsible for reviewing these metrics weekly. If the model or user base changes, re-run the evaluation.

**Note:** Legal requirements regarding automated decisions affecting minors must be routed to counsel; this strategy addresses product safety, not legal compliance. Do not claim the system is "unbiased"; report the measured residual risk and let the leadership make the ship decision.
